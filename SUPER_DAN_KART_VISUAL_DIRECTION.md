# Super Dan Kart — visual direction
## Reference and intent
The supplied retro kart-racing screenshot shows a low camera behind the kart, a textured ground plane receding to a flat horizon, chunky scaled racer sprites, saturated grass, tan dirt, colorful curb blocks, a high-contrast timer, and a separate live course overview. Translate those compositional principles into Dan's original world. The target is a playful early-console racing aesthetic, not a recreation of another game's characters or exact course art.
## Screen hierarchy
The main viewport is a 256 × 176 pixel canvas, expanded sharply without smoothing. Horizon sits around the upper quarter; the player's rear-view kart is anchored in the lower foreground. Opponents shrink toward the horizon. White/cream pixel-like numerals with dark outlines remain readable over any theme. Display the course map immediately below the race view, with six color-coded dots and a highlighted Dan marker. Cream/orange mobile controls remain part of the familiar Dan Likes Chips shell.
## Art rules
Use bold stepped silhouettes, compact rectangular highlights, dark brown outlines, flat shadows, alternating dirt/road tiles, scattered ground flecks, and bright segmented curbs. Avoid modern bloom, photorealism, smooth gradients, or directly copied game assets. Maintain the red beetle identity, original garage colors, costumes and chip motif. The rear body of each kart reflects its vehicle category, with wheels or hovering runners as appropriate; steering and drift lean the sprite. Boost flames and sparks use a few warm pixels, not distracting overlays. Keep readability and touch targets above decoration.

## Animation and feedback
Countdown and GO appear centered. Chip pickups flash briefly; hazards generate readable feedback. Drift sparks and boost exhaust are anchored to the kart. Background scenery shifts subtly with camera heading. Minimap updates continuously. Avoid rapid full-screen flashing and respect reduced-motion where applicable.
## Delivery
Original Canvas-generated artwork and reused original Dan character assets only. The visual implementation should be judged from actual gameplay, not from an illustrative mockup. Verify the real mobile display before claiming final visual acceptance.
